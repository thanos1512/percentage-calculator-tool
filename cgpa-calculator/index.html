How GPA is calculated

Each grade carries grade points. Multiply each course's grade points by its credit hours to get quality points, add them up, and divide by the total credit hours.

Example. A in a 3-credit course (3 × 4.00 = 12), B+ in a 3-credit course (3 × 3.33 = 9.99) and B in a 2-credit lab (2 × 3.00 = 6). Quality points: 27.99. Credit hours: 8. GPA = 27.99 ÷ 8 = 3.49.

How CGPA is calculated

CGPA is the GPA across every semester, weighted by credit hours. Multiply your current CGPA by the credit hours you have completed, add this semester's quality points, and divide by the new total of credit hours.

Example. CGPA 3.20 over 60 credit hours, then a semester GPA of 3.60 over 18 credit hours: (3.20 × 60 + 3.60 × 18) ÷ 78 = 256.8 ÷ 78 = 3.29.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between GPA and CGPA?

GPA is for one semester. CGPA (cumulative GPA) covers every semester you have studied so far, weighted by credit hours.

What CGPA do I need to avoid probation?

Most universities place students on probation below 2.00 CGPA, and many require at least 2.00 to graduate with a bachelor's degree and 2.50 for a master's. Check your university's rules.

Do labs count in CGPA?

Yes, a lab with its own credit hours and grade counts like any other course, weighted by its credit hours.

Can I convert CGPA to percentage?

There is no single formula used by every university. Many publish their own conversion table on the transcript or in the prospectus, and HEC degree attestation uses the CGPA itself. Use your university's table.

How do repeated courses affect CGPA?

Most universities replace the old grade with the new one when you repeat a course. Leave the old attempt out and enter only the new grade.
